Questions students ask

Account, billing and platform questions live in the Help Center.

What is an externship?
A remote externship is a new form of professional work experience with learning built in. We don’t do simulations or busy work: real projects, real companies, real impact.
What will I do in my externship?
You dive into a strategic business problem defined by the company you’re externing with. You get to know the industry and the problem space, study best practices and learn from experts. Then you put together a concrete deliverable that addresses the core business problem and present it to the company.
How much time does an externship take?
Plan on about 10 to 15 hours a week, covering learning, training, research and time with peers and professionals. Externships run from a few weeks to a couple of months; each listing shows its length. Outside the scheduled group sessions, you do it all on your own schedule.
How flexible is an externship?
Externships are fully remote and built to fit a busy schedule, whether you’re in school, working full-time or even on vacation. Most of the work is asynchronous through the Extern platform, and externship meetings are recorded. A few programs have small exceptions, noted in the program details.
Am I eligible?
When we say anyone, we mean anyone. Whether you’re in college, taking classes while working, recently graduated or making a switch in your career, you’re welcome. Just read the program details and make sure you can commit 10 to 15 hours a week.
When can I start?
Whenever you’re ready. Externships open on a rolling basis: sign up, pick one that fits and start working on it. If a program doesn’t fit your schedule, you can move to another externship that matches your ambitions.
Will doing an externship help me get hired?
An externship is what you make of it, but many externs say it was pivotal in their hiring process: 74% of externs find a job outcome within a year. Most entry-level postings still ask for experience, and it takes experience to get experience. Nothing guarantees an offer, but real work on your resume signals to recruiters that you’ve built skills that matter in the workplace.
Is an externship the same as shadowing?
No. Externships put you on real business projects offered by partner companies. You get mentorship and training as experts introduce the project, then complete assignments and a final presentation or report according to a curriculum.
